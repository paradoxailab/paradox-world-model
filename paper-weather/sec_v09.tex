\section{Version 0.9: how much data, and a new encoder}\label{sec:v09}

\subsection{Data threshold}

How much of a network's own history does \pwm need to match the foundation models, which use no training data?
For each budget of $M$ months, we train \pwm on only the last $M$ months before the validation period. The training
windows, their inputs and every normalisation statistic all lie inside the budget, and no partner network is used.
The setting is fixed in advance: P8 without cross-network training (width 128, 3 layers, 14-day encoder, weight
averaging), the mean of 3 seeds. The fixed validation period picks only the training checkpoint, so ``$M$ months''
means $M$ months of training data plus that period. Stations, test windows and significance tests are the league's.

The answer is clear at the airports (Table~\ref{tab:threshold}). With 12 months of history \pwm mostly ties. With
24 months it loses nowhere. With 48 months it beats all three foundation models on every variable. The WMO stations, with more
stations and more varied sites, get there faster: no losses at 12 months and wins everywhere at 24. On the CPCB
sensors, 16 months of local history are not enough: training on the network alone, it still loses on almost every
comparison. Cross-network training (v0.6) and the later encoders recover much of that gap, as shown below. At the airports,
the step from 12 to 48 months moved every comparison by 4--7 percentage points; on CPCB, PWM-X's encoder moved the
same comparisons by 0.6--1.6 points.

\subsection{PWM-X: a 21-day patch encoder}

PWM-X adds a second encoder to each station token. It reads the station's last 21 days as 21 daily patches of
the three targets and their observed flags. Each window is normalised by its own masked mean and standard deviation,
which is how the foundation models see a series. A two-layer transformer encodes the patches, and attention pooling
plus the last patch feed a zero-initialised projection added to the token. P10 also hides 15\% of the patches during
training and reconstructs them, a self-supervised loss with weight 0.1. P9 has 1,251,709 parameters and P10
1,260,997. Both run on top of P4 (cross-network training) with weight averaging.

On CPCB validation, P9 scored 0.4606 and P10 0.4597, against P8's 0.4678 and P0's 0.4922 $\pm$ 0.0038; the rule
chose P10. Its margin over P8 (0.0081) only just exceeds $2\sigma$ (0.0076). The hybrid on P10 chose H1 (validation
0.4506; H2 0.4607, H3 0.4958), slightly worse than v0.8's hybrid (0.4444).

\subsection{A prospective real-time track}

A live version of the pure model, trained on the six airports up to 23 September 2026 and validated to 7 November,
issues a 24-hour forecast from the latest airport reports and logs it before the weather happens. Every logged
hour is later scored against the reports. The first forecast was issued on 9 November 2026 at 06 UTC, so no scores
exist yet. This track is the clean test that the reused test periods of this study cannot give.
